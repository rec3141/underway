# Welcome

The underway dashboard is a web page about the research icebreaker CCGS
*Amundsen*. It shows where the ship is and where she has been. It shows what
her instruments measure, minute by minute. It also shows the stations she
works, the casts she makes, her schedule, and the history and nature of the
waters she sails in.

You open it in a web browser, like any other web page. You do not need to
install anything, and you do not need an account.

## Who it is for

The dashboard is for everyone on board and everyone following the ship from
shore:

- **Scientists** who want to see the water their samples came from, compare
  casts, or check what the ship measured while they slept.
- **Crew** who want to know where the ship is, what comes next on the
  schedule, or what the weather station reads.
- **Students** writing their part of the cruise report.
- **Visitors** and families ashore who want to follow the voyage.

You do not need to know anything about oceanography to use it. This manual
explains every word that a newcomer would not know.

## What you can do with it

Here is the dashboard as it first opens on a computer screen. The map of the
ship's track is on the left. The panels with the latest readings are on the
right.

![The dashboard on a computer, showing the 2026 season: the map on the left, the latest readings on the right](img/01-overview.webp)

With the dashboard you can:

- **Follow the ship on a map.** The ship's track is drawn as a line of
  coloured dots. You choose what the colour means: sea temperature, salinity
  (how salty the water is), wind, depth and more. See [The map](map.html).
- **Read the underway measurements.** "Underway" means measured continuously
  while the ship moves: the water pumped in through the hull, the weather on
  the mast, the ship's speed and heading. See [The Underway tab](underway.html).
- **Spot unusual moments.** The *surprise score* flags minutes when the
  readings look unlike the hours before. See [The surprise score](surprise.html).
- **Look at casts.** A *cast* is one lowering of instruments into the water
  at a station, from the surface down towards the sea floor. You can draw one
  cast, compare several, or line them up as a section. See
  [The Casts tab](casts.html).
- **Find stations.** A *station* is a place where the ship stops to work.
  See [The Stations tab](stations.html).
- **Check the schedule.** See what is happening now and what comes next. See
  [The Schedule tab](schedule.html).
- **Measure distances and mark places.** Drop a *waypoint* anywhere on the
  map and see how far it is from the ship, by sea and by air. See
  [Waypoints, distances and exporting the map](waypoints-and-export.html).
- **Save maps and graphs as pictures**, or the map as a file for Google
  Earth. See [Waypoints, distances and exporting the map](waypoints-and-export.html)
  and [Tables and downloads](tables-and-downloads.html).
- **Download the numbers** as tables. See [Tables and downloads](tables-and-downloads.html).
- **Browse photos** from the ship's cameras and from people on board. See
  [The Photos tab](photos.html).
- **Read about the region** in the Wiki: past voyages, people and places, and
  the animals, ice and weather. See [The Wiki tab](wiki.html).
- **Chat** with others on board, and ask questions of the dashboard's AI
  crew. See [Chat and games](chat-and-games.html).
- **Build your team's cruise report.** See [The cruise report builder](cruise-report.html).

The dashboard covers more than this cruise. It also holds the ship's earlier
seasons, back to 2002, from the Amundsen Science archive. You can show any of
them on the same map and graphs. See [Choosing legs and a time span](legs-and-time.html).

## Aboard and ashore

The dashboard runs on a computer on the ship. On the ship's network you see
everything, with new data arriving every few minutes.

A copy of the dashboard is also published on the public web, for people
ashore. That copy shows the same map, graphs and casts, but some things work
only aboard: the chat, photo uploads, alerts, feedback and the live feeds.
[Opening the dashboard](opening.html) explains the difference.

When a cruise is over, the dashboard says so at the top of the page: "cruise
ended" and the date. You can still look at everything the ship recorded.

## How this manual is laid out

The manual has two parts. This part, the user manual, is for people who use
the dashboard. A second part, the administrator manual, is for the people who
install and run it.

The user manual goes from the general to the particular:

1. The first chapters ([Opening the dashboard](opening.html),
   [A tour of the screen](tour.html)) show you around.
2. [Choosing legs and a time span](legs-and-time.html) explains the two
   controls that decide what every tab shows. Read it before the others.
3. The map gets two chapters of its own, because it sits beside every tab.
4. Then there is one chapter for each tab, in the order the tabs appear.
5. The last chapter is a glossary: a list of the words the dashboard uses,
   with their meanings, and answers to common questions.

You do not have to read it in order. Each chapter stands on its own, and
links to the others where they help.

## How to read this manual

**Bold words** are the words you see on the page, written exactly as the page
writes them: a button, a menu, a label. For example: click **Legs**, then
tick **2026 Leg 3**.

Numbered lists are tasks. Do the steps in order. Each step says what to click
and what you then see.

Pictures show what the text describes. They come from the dashboard itself,
with the real data of the 2026 season. Your screen may look a little
different: the data change, and you may have chosen another colour theme or
text size.

Some paragraphs are set apart in boxes:

!!! tip
    A tip is a shortcut or a good habit. You can skip it.

!!! note
    A note is something worth knowing, such as a limit or an exception.

!!! note "Aboard only"
    A box like this one marks something that works only on the ship's
    network, not on the public web copy.

Times in the dashboard are *ship time*: the time zone set for the ship's
dashboard (Eastern time, shown as EDT, in 2026). The page always shows the
zone's short name beside a time, so you can tell.
