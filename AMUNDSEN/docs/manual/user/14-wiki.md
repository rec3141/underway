# The Wiki tab

The **Wiki** tab is a library about the region the ship sails through. It has
two halves:

- **History**: the human past of the Arctic, from the Tuniit and the Thule to
  the ships of the last century. Voyages and their routes, winterings,
  shipwrecks, people, places, old maps, photographs, quotes from journals.
- **Nature**: what the Arctic is and does. The rock, the glaciers, the sea ice,
  the water, the weather, the sky and the living things, as people recorded
  them over the centuries, and as the ship records them now.

Everything in the wiki is sourced: each page and picture says where it came
from. Much of it is on the map too, so you can see what happened where the
ship is today.

A **wiki** is a set of pages linked to one another. Click a blue name in any
page to go to that name's own page.

!!! note
    The wiki works aboard and on the public web copy. A few things work only
    aboard: **Ask a Q** (the chat), the review flags **⚑**, and the ship's own
    photos (see [The Photos tab](photos.html)).

## Opening the wiki

1. Click **Wiki** in the row of tabs at the top.
2. The right-hand side shows the wiki's front page, **Home**. The map stays on
   the left, and shows the wiki's places when you turn on its layers (see
   [The wiki on the map](#the-wiki-on-the-map)).

While the **Wiki** tab is open, the ship's own map layers (**Track**,
**Stations** and **Events**) step aside, so the map shows the wiki. They come
back when you leave the tab.

## The tool bar

A row of buttons sits at the top of the wiki.

![The wiki's tool bar: Back, Home, Explore, the History and Nature labels, Ask a Q, the close button, the search box and the Browse menu](img/14-tools.webp)

- **‹ Back** goes back to the page you saw before. Your browser's own back
  button does the same.
- **Home** opens the front page.
- **Explore** lists every topic. Click it again to go home.
- **History** and **Nature** are labels that filter the wiki (see
  [History, Nature or both](#history-nature-or-both)).
- **Ask a Q** opens the ship's chat, to ask a question (aboard only; see
  [Asking a question](#asking-a-question)).
- **×** closes the wiki and returns to the tab you were on before.
- The **search** box: type a word to search the whole wiki.
- **Browse…**: a menu of every kind of page (see
  [Browsing by kind](#browsing-by-kind)).

## The front page

The front page changes every day and with every move of the ship.

![The wiki's front page: On this day, In this place, Observed on this day, Observed near the ship](img/14-home.webp)

It starts with four cards:

- **On this day · October 16**: things in the history that happened on
  today's date in other years, for example "1944 St. Roch, Halifax to
  Vancouver by Lancaster Sound in one season, 1944 ended". Five are picked
  each day; **see 100 more** lists them all.
- **In this place · Cambridge Bay**: things in the history close to where the
  ship is now, nearest first, with the distance, for example "1 km USCGC
  Storis on the western DEW Line sealift". The name in the title is where the
  ship is.
- **Observed on this day**: entries of the nature record made on today's date,
  for example a sea-ice thickness measured in 1906.
- **Observed near the ship**: nature observations close to the ship, for
  example the caribou and muskox seen around Cambridge Bay.

Click a line to open its page. A line with a **⌖** also moves the map to that
spot.

Below the cards:

- **/Share Photos**: the five newest entries of the ship's own journal, with
  **open** to see them all and **Add photos** to add yours (aboard only; see
  [The Photos tab](photos.html)).
- **Domains**: one button per field of nature (**Biology**, **Geology**,
  **Glaciology**, **Sea ice**, **Oceanography**, **Hydrography**,
  **Meteorology**, **Astronomy**, **Geomagnetism**, **Freshwater**), with how
  many observations each has. **All observations** lists them all.
- **Narratives**: one card per topic, each with a picture. Click one to open
  the topic.

## Explore: the topics

The wiki is organised in **topics**: a voyage, an era, a region, a field of
nature. Each topic has a few **narrative pages** (written stories) and many
**artifacts** (the photographs, maps, quotes, texts, objects, routes and
events behind them).

Click **Explore** to see them all.

![Explore: the number of topics, pages and artifacts, then a card per topic](img/14-explore.webp)

Each card shows the topic's picture, title, a summary, and its number of pages
and artifacts, for example **Amundsen's Gjoa, 1903-1906 · 7 pages · 90
artifacts**. The coloured stripe on the left is the topic's colour, used for
its dots on the map and in lists.

When the **Nature** half is shown, Explore ends with **Kinds of subject**:
**Taxa** (living things), **Rock units**, **Fossils**, **Minerals**,
**Landforms**, **Ice**, **Water**, **Weather**, **Sky**, **Field**,
**Phenomena**.

### A topic's page

Click a topic card to open it.

![A topic's page: its picture, title and summary, then its narrative pages and artifacts](img/14-topic.webp)

A topic's page has its picture (with credit), a summary, the list of its
narrative pages, and then all its artifacts as cards, the ones the stories
mention first. A topic marked **draft** is still being written.

### A narrative page

Click a narrative page to read it.

![A narrative page: the story, with the artifacts it mentions set beside the text](img/14-page.webp)

- The line above the title is the **trail**, for example **Wiki › Amundsen's
  Gjoa, 1903-1906 › EXPLORE**. Click a part of it to go there.
- Blue names in the text link to the pages of people, places, ships and
  artifacts.
- The artifacts the text mentions sit beside it, as cards: a map, a route, a
  photograph. Click a card to open it. Click **map ↗** on a card to show the
  place on the map.
- At the end, **People** and **Places** list everyone and everywhere the page
  names, and **Mentioned in** lists the pages that link to this one.

### An artifact's page

An **artifact** is one thing in the record: a photograph, a map, a quote, a
text, an object, a route or an event.

![An artifact's page: a photograph, its kind, date, position and source, with gallery navigation](img/14-artifact.webp)

Its page shows:

- its kind, date and position (click the position or **map ↗** to show it on
  the map);
- its **source**: the book, archive or website it came from;
- the picture itself, with its credit and its rights (who may reuse it);
- links to the ship's own copy (**on the ship**), the original file, and the
  holding institution's copy (**on the web ↗**);
- for a route, a drawing of it and a table of its dated positions.

On a photograph's page, **← Gallery**, **3 / 63**, **‹** and **›** move
through the topic's pictures, as in the photo gallery. Click a picture to see
it large.

## Searching

1. Click in the **search** box at the top.
2. Type a word or two, for example `Gjoa Haven`. The results appear as you
   type.

![Search results for Gjoa Haven: the pages, then the images](img/14-search.webp)

The results are grouped by kind (**Pages**, **Images**, **People**,
**Places**, **Events** …), with how many of each. The ones whose title
matches come first. When the **Nature** half is shown, the subjects and
observations found follow, with a table.

Every word you type must be found. Type more words to narrow the search. To
clear it, click the **×** at the right of the box.

When a topic is open, the search looks inside that topic only.

## Browsing by kind

The **Browse…** menu at the right of the search box opens a page of
everything of one kind:

- **Pages**: **Explore**; **Bibliography**, every work the wiki cites, which
  you can download as BibTeX, RIS, CSL-JSON or plain text; **Provenance**, how
  the wiki was made.
- **Images**, and **/Share Photos** (the ship's own photos, aboard only).
- **Events**: the timeline of every dated event, and **Observations**: the
  whole nature record.
- **People**: **All people**, **Animals**, **Objects**, **Quotes**,
  **Texts**.
- **Places**: **All places**, **Vessels**, **Tracks** (routes), **Maps**.
- **Domains** (when Nature is shown): one page per field of nature.

People, animals, ships and places are listed alphabetically. Images, maps,
quotes, texts, objects and tracks are grouped in **collections**, one per
topic: click a collection to open its cards.

### The Events timeline

**Browse…** → **Events** draws every dated event of the wiki on a chart: one
row per topic, a dot for a date, a bar for a span of time (a wintering, a
voyage).

![The Events timeline: a row per topic, dots and bars against the years, and the table below](img/14-events.webp)

- The chart opens at the year 1400. Scroll on it to zoom, drag to move along
  the years. The **⟲** button shows the whole range.
- Click a dot or bar to jump to its row in the table below.
- The table lists every event with its **Date**, **What** and **Where**.

### A domain's page

A **domain** is a field of nature, for example **Sea ice**. Open one from the
front page's **Domains**, or from **Browse…**.

![The Sea ice domain: its parts as tiles](img/14-domain.webp)

A domain's page has a tile for each of its parts: **Explore** (its topics and
pages), **Images**, **Events**, **Maps**, **Glossary** (its subjects, for
example the kinds of ice) and **Observations** (its record, with a chart).

A **subject** is a thing that can be observed: a species, a rock, a kind of
ice, the aurora. Its page gives its names in English, French, Inuktitut and
Kalaallisut where known, its pictures, and every observation of it, with a
chart of the values over the years.

An **observation** is one record of a subject: one date, one place, one
source, for example "pack ice, 18 feet, 1906, Joseph-Elzéar Bernier".

## History, Nature or both

The two labels **History** and **Nature** in the tool bar choose what the wiki
shows:

- Neither on (the start): both halves.
- **History** on: only the human past.
- **Nature** on: only the natural record.
- Both on: both halves.

Click a label to turn it on or off. A label that is on is highlighted. The
labels change only the wiki pane, not the map.

## Asking a question

**Ask a Q** opens the ship's chat beside the wiki, in a room where the ship's
AI crew answer questions from the wiki:

- With **History** alone on, the button reads **Ask Ada**, and opens the
  **Library**, where Ada, the librarian, answers about the region's past.
- With **Nature** alone on, it reads **Ask Doc**, and opens Doc's room, the
  **Lab**, where Doc, the naturalist, answers about the living things, the
  ice, the water and the sky.
- Otherwise it reads **Ask a Q** and opens the **Deck**, where Ada and Doc
  both answer.

The page you are reading is passed on with your question, so you can ask
"what happened to them after this?" Their answers link to the wiki pages they
used. See [Chat and games](chat-and-games.html).

!!! note "Aboard only"
    The chat and its AI crew run on the ship. On the public web copy,
    **Ask a Q** is not shown.

## Flagging a page for review

The wiki is written by people and a computer model, and can be wrong. If you
see a mistake, flag it.

1. Click the small flag **⚑** after a page's title, or in the corner of an
   artifact's card.
2. A box asks **What should be looked at? (optional)**. Type what is wrong, for
   example "this photo is of Gjoa Haven, not Cambridge Bay".
3. Click **OK**. The flag turns dark. Everyone aboard now sees it, and the
   wiki's keeper is told.

Hold the mouse over a flag to see who raised it and why. A number after it,
**⚑2**, means two people did. To withdraw your own flag, click it again while
yours is the only one.

For problems with the dashboard itself (a button that does not work, a graph
that looks wrong), use **Feedback** at the top of the page instead (see
[Sources, feedback and help](sources-and-help.html)).

!!! note "Aboard only"
    Flags are kept on the ship's server. They are not shown on the public web
    copy.

## The wiki on the map

Two pills above the map show the wiki on it:

- **History**: the history's artifacts and places.
- **Nature**: the nature record's observations.

Click a pill to turn it on (dark blue). A menu appears beside it, to choose
what to show.

![The map with the History layer on and its Kinds menu open](img/14-map-history.webp)

- Beside **History**, **Kinds · 8/8** lists the kinds of artifact (**Images**,
  **Events**, **Objects**, **Quotes**, **Texts**, **Places**, **Tracks**,
  **Maps**), each with its colour. Untick a kind to hide it. **all** and
  **none** tick or untick them all.
- Beside **Nature**, **Domains** lists the fields of nature in the same way.

On the map:

- Each artifact is a dot in its kind's colour; a route is a line.
- Hold the mouse over a dot to see what it is. Where several things share a
  spot, all are listed.
- Click a dot to open its page in the wiki. Where several things share the
  spot, a list opens so you can choose.
- When a topic is open in the wiki, the map shows only that topic's
  artifacts.

**Example**: open the topic **Amundsen's Gjoa, 1903-1906**, turn on
**History**, and the map shows the Gjøa's route through the Northwest Passage,
the photographs taken at Gjoa Haven, and the places she wintered.

## In short

- **Wiki** is a sourced library of the region's history and nature, with the
  map beside it.
- The front page shows what happened on this day and near the ship.
- **Explore** lists the topics; **search** finds any word; **Browse…** opens
  everything of one kind.
- **History** and **Nature** filter the pane; the map pills of the same names
  show the wiki on the map.
- Aboard, **Ask a Q** asks the AI crew, and **⚑** flags a mistake.
