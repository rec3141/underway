# Glossary and questions

## Words used in the dashboard

ACSD
:   The ship's own underway record: the file the ship's data system writes each
    day with the readings of its instruments (the TSG, the weather station,
    the navigation, the winches). It is written in batches every ten minutes.
    The dashboard reads it. See [A tour of the screen](tour.html).

ADCP, LADCP
:   An ADCP (acoustic Doppler current profiler) measures the speed and
    direction of the water currents with sound. An **LADCP** (lowered ADCP)
    rides on the rosette and measures the currents from the surface to the
    bottom during a CTD cast. See [The Casts tab](casts.html).

AI crew
:   The four characters who answer in the ship chat: Cap'n Barnacle, Doc, Ada
    and Polly. They are one computer language model playing four parts. See
    [Chat and games](chat-and-games.html).

Archive
:   The data of earlier seasons, 2002 to 2024, published by Amundsen Science.
    The archive's legs are marked **archive** in the **Legs** menu. See
    [Choosing legs and a time span](legs-and-time.html).

Arrival on station
:   In the cruise report builder, the moment the ship slowed below 1 knot
    before its first deployment at a station. The station's conditions are
    taken then.

Artifact
:   In the wiki, one thing in the record: a photograph, a map, a quote, a
    text, an object, a route or an event. See [The Wiki tab](wiki.html).

Bathymetry
:   The depth and shape of the sea floor, drawn on the map in colours. See
    [The map](map.html).

Bottle
:   One of the water bottles on the rosette. Each is closed ("fired") at a
    chosen depth to bring water up. **Bottles fired** lists them for each
    cast. See [The Casts tab](casts.html).

Cast
:   One lowering of an instrument into the sea and back, for example one CTD
    cast with the rosette.

CDOM
:   Coloured dissolved organic matter: material from land and from living
    things, dissolved in the water, that colours it brown. River water has a
    lot of it.

CIS, ice chart
:   The Canadian Ice Service, which draws daily and regional charts of the
    sea ice. The **Ice charts** layer shows them on the map.

CTD
:   An instrument that measures **c**onductivity (and so salinity),
    **t**emperature and **d**epth (pressure) as it is lowered. On the
    Amundsen it is mounted on the rosette, with other sensors.

Cruise report
:   The report each science team writes before the end of its leg, in
    Amundsen Science's Word template. See
    [The cruise report builder](cruise-report.html).

Derived
:   A value the dashboard calculates from measured ones, for example the
    surprise score or the excess heat. The **Sources** tab marks it *derived*.

Dip
:   One profile of an MVP tow: the profiler falls through the water and is
    pulled back up. One tow holds many dips.

Domain
:   In the wiki, a field of nature: Biology, Geology, Glaciology, Sea ice,
    Oceanography, Hydrography, Meteorology, Astronomy, Geomagnetism,
    Freshwater.

Draft
:   In the cruise report builder, your report as it stands, saved on the
    ship's server so you or your team can open it again.

Event label
:   The name of one operation in the event log, for example `AMD2603-010`:
    the ship (AMD), the year and leg (`2603` is 2026, Leg 3), and a number.

Event log
:   The bridge's record of every operation: each cast, net, core or flight,
    with its time and position. See [The Schedule tab](schedule.html).

Excess heat
:   How far the sea surface temperature is above the temperature at which
    that water would freeze. Near zero, the water is at its freezing point
    and ice can form.

Feedback
:   A message about the dashboard, sent with the **Feedback** button. See
    [Sources, feedback and help](sources-and-help.html).

Flag (⚑)
:   A mark that asks for a wiki page or photo to be checked. See
    [The Wiki tab](wiki.html#flagging-a-page-for-review).

Fluorescence
:   Light given off by the chlorophyll of microscopic algae. It shows how much
    algae (phytoplankton) is in the water, in µg/L.

Heave, sea state
:   How far the ship rises and falls with the waves. **Sea state · 4σ heave
    (m)** is close to the height of the larger waves.

ICS
:   A calendar file format. An ICS link adds the ship's schedule to your own
    calendar app. See [The Schedule tab](schedule.html).

Import
:   Adding photos to the ship's journal: the server reads each photo, places
    it on the ship's track and describes it. See [The Photos tab](photos.html).

Intranet
:   The ship's own internal web, reachable only on the ship's network.

Journal
:   The ship's own record of what people aboard have seen, mostly
    photographs. Shown as **/Share Photos**. See [The Photos tab](photos.html).

KMZ
:   A map file for Google Earth. The map can be exported as one, and a
    cruise plan can be loaded from one. See
    [Waypoints, distances and exporting the map](waypoints-and-export.html).

Knot (kn)
:   A speed of one nautical mile per hour, about 1.85 km/h.

Leg
:   One part of a season, usually three to five weeks, between two changes of
    the science teams. **2026 Leg 3** is the third leg of 2026. See
    [Choosing legs and a time span](legs-and-time.html).

Live
:   Data arriving now. **LIVE** in the status line means the newest
    observation is less than 15 minutes old. The **Live** cast is the one in
    the water now.

Logsheet, log
:   In the cruise report builder, your team's own records: a spreadsheet you
    kept, or a logbook page you photographed and transcribed.

MVP
:   Moving Vessel Profiler: a small profiler dropped and pulled up again and
    again from behind the moving ship, giving many profiles (dips) along its
    path.

Nautical mile (nmi)
:   1.852 km. Used at sea for distances.

Observation
:   In the wiki's nature half, one record of a subject: one date, one place,
    one source. A photo in the journal is one too.

Operation
:   One line of the event log: one cast, core, net tow, flight or other piece
    of work.

Plan
:   The planned route and stations of a leg, from the expedition's KMZ file,
    shown by the **Plan** layer on the map.

Profile
:   Measurements from the surface down, drawn as a line against depth. A CTD
    cast gives one profile per variable.

PSU
:   Practical salinity unit. Ocean water is about 35 PSU; fresh water is 0.
    Arctic surface water is often 25 to 32 PSU, fresher where rivers and
    melting ice add water.

Rosette
:   The frame lowered on a cable from the ship, carrying the CTD, other
    sensors and 24 water bottles. **ROS** in the casts list.

Salinity
:   How salty the water is, in PSU.

Satellite pictures (Sat)
:   Recent pictures of the sea around the ship from the Sentinel satellites:
    Sentinel-1 radar, which sees the ice through cloud, and Sentinel-2 true
    colour. The **Sat** pill shows them on the map. See [The map](map.html).

Section
:   Several casts side by side, drawn as a slice through the sea with depth
    downwards and distance (or time) across. See [The Casts tab](casts.html).

Share, /Share
:   The ship's shared network drive, where everyone keeps their files.

Ship time
:   The time on the ship's clocks (Eastern time on the 2026 season). The
    dashboard shows times in ship time; downloads are in UTC.

SST
:   Sea surface temperature: the temperature of the water at the ship's
    intake, near the surface.

SSS
:   Sea surface salinity.

Station
:   A place where the ship stops to work, with a name such as `GF-1` or
    `CardS-3`. See [The Stations tab](stations.html).

Subject
:   In the wiki's nature half, something that can be observed: a species, a
    rock, a kind of ice, the aurora.

Surprise score
:   A number that says how unusual the sea surface water is, compared with
    the hours before. See [The surprise score](surprise.html).

T–S diagram
:   A graph of temperature against salinity. Each kind of water mass has its
    own place on it.

Timelapse
:   A short film made from a whole day of one camera's photos. Shown as a
    camera symbol on the map.

TM
:   The trace-metal rosette: a separate, metal-free rosette for sampling
    trace metals such as iron.

Topic
:   In the wiki, one subject of study (a voyage, an era, a region, a field of
    nature), with its narrative pages and artifacts.

Track
:   The line the ship has sailed, on the map.

Transect, TRS
:   A line of stations across a strait, a shelf or a current, sampled one
    after another. A group of casts saved as a section is a **TRS**.

TSG
:   Thermosalinograph: the instrument in the ship's lab that measures the
    temperature and salinity of the sea water pumped in from the hull.

TSV
:   Tab-separated values: a text file of a table, which any spreadsheet can
    open. The **TSV** buttons download one.

Underway
:   Measured continuously while the ship sails, as opposed to at stations.

UTC
:   Coordinated Universal Time, the world's reference clock. Downloads are in
    UTC. Eastern daylight time is UTC − 4 hours.

Waypoint
:   A point you drop on the map to measure distances and depths. See
    [Waypoints, distances and exporting the map](waypoints-and-export.html).

Wiki
:   A set of linked pages. The **Wiki** tab is the ship's library of the
    region's history and nature.

## Common questions

### Why does the page say "data 15 d ago" or "cruise ended"?

No new measurements have arrived for that long. **cruise ended** means the
season is over and the dashboard shows the last data it received. Aboard,
an age of more than an hour means something upstream has stopped: tell
someone with **Feedback**. See [A tour of the screen](tour.html).

### Why do the times not match my watch?

The dashboard shows ship time. Downloaded files and the cruise report
builder's operations list use UTC. Check which one a page says.

### Why can I not see the chat, the photos or the Feedback button?

You are on the public web copy, ashore. The chat, the photos, photo uploads,
feedback, wiki flags and alerts work only on the ship's network. See
[Opening the dashboard](opening.html).

### Why is the map, or a graph, empty?

The legs and time span you chose may hold no data for it. Choose more legs
or a longer span. Archive legs carry fewer measurements than this year's. See
[Choosing legs and a time span](legs-and-time.html).

### Why are some points grey?

The ship's seawater pump was off or slowed, so the lab's readings were not of
the sea. See [The Underway tab](underway.html).

### Can I download the data?

Yes. Every table and list has a **TSV** button, and every graph can be saved
as a picture. See [Tables and downloads](tables-and-downloads.html).

### How do I cite the data?

Cite Amundsen Science / ArcticNet. For the archive, cite each dataset's DOI
from the Amundsen Science catalogue; the archive is licensed CC BY 4.0. The
**Sources** tab has the details.

### Who can read what I write in the chat?

Anyone on the dashboard can read the shared rooms (**Mess**, **Lounge**,
**Library**, **Deck**). Private conversations are kept in memory only and
erased after 30 minutes of quiet. See [Chat and games](chat-and-games.html).

### Are the AI crew's answers right?

Not always. They read the dashboard and the wiki and can misread them. Check
anything that matters.

### Where did my cruise report go?

Your browser keeps the report you were working on, on that computer. To open
it on another computer, you must first click **Save draft**; then choose it
from the **Draft** menu. See [The cruise report builder](cruise-report.html).

### Does the dashboard need the internet?

No. Aboard, it runs on the ship's own server, and the charts, tables, map and
wiki work without the internet. A few things may use the ship's satellite
link: the satellite pictures, the transcription of logbook photos, the public
web copy, and, depending on how the ship is set up, the AI crew and the photo
descriptions.
